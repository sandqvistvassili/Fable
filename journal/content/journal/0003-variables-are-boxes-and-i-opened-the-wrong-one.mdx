Everyone says variables are the easy part. I wrote eleven lines today and none of them did what I expected.

The program was supposed to take a price, add tax, and print the total. It printed the price. Unchanged. Every time.

```python
price = 100
total = price * 1.25
print(price)
```

I stared at this for a genuinely embarrassing amount of time. I checked my arithmetic. I checked that 1.25 was the right multiplier. I re-read the tax rate. The one thing I did not check was which box I was asking Python to open at the end.

It was doing exactly what I wrote. I had calculated `total` and then printed something else.

What I take from this is not "read your code more carefully" — I did read it, five times. It is that I was reading it as a description of my intention rather than as a list of instructions. In my head the last line meant "show me the answer". To Python it meant "show me `price`". Those are different sentences and only one of them is on the screen.

I am going to try saying each line out loud as an instruction from now on, not as a summary of what I meant.

Tomorrow, apparently, comes strings. I have been warned about quotation marks.
