Security is where I want to end up eventually, so today I opened a free web security lab to see what the destination looks like from here.

I understood almost none of it.

The exercise involved changing part of a web request to make a site behave differently. I could follow the individual words. I could not follow why any of it worked, because I do not yet know what a request is, what a parameter is, or what the site was supposed to do with either. It was like being handed a car engine before I had learned there was such a thing as a car.

I stopped after forty minutes, which I want to record as a decision rather than a failure. The gap was not "this is difficult". The gap was "I am missing the layer underneath".

Two things I am taking from it anyway.

The first is a corrected route. Before security comes the web: what a request is, what a response is, what a server does with the two. That is a prerequisite I did not know I was missing this morning.

The second is calibration. Nobody hires a beginner into security — the normal path is a few years as a developer first. Today made that concrete rather than theoretical. The destination is real, it is far, and the road to it goes through becoming a competent programmer, not around it.

Back to Python tomorrow.
