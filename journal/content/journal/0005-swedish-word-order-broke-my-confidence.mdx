Swedish is not optional for me. I need it for admission to Yrkeshögskola, which means it is graded, and a grade is not something enthusiasm can fix at the last minute. So it gets real hours, not leftover ones.

Today was word order, and specifically the rule that the verb wants to be in second position. On paper I can do this. Give me a sentence and thirty seconds and I will put *igår* first and the verb second and get it right.

> Igår läste jag en bok.

Out loud, in a conversation, with someone waiting for me to finish — I put the verb wherever Russian or English would have put it. Every time. The knowledge is there and it does not arrive fast enough to be useful.

What I noticed today is that this is exactly the same problem as reading my own Python and seeing what I meant instead of what I wrote. In both cases I know the rule and my instinct overrides it before the rule gets a chance to speak.

The fix is presumably the same too: repetition until the correct version is the instinct, not the correction. Which is slow, and not very interesting to write about, and is going to be most of the next year in both subjects.

Small comfort: Swedish and English are related closely enough that half the vocabulary comes free. It is only the grammar that is out to get me.
