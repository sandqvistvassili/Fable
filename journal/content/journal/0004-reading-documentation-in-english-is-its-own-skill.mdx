I thought my English was good enough for this. Then I opened the official Python documentation.

It is not that the words are hard. It is that the sentences are built differently. News writing explains as it goes. Documentation assumes you already know the shape of what is being described and only tells you the parameters. A line like "returns a shallow copy of the list" contains three ideas I had to look up separately, and none of them were the difficult one.

The difficult one was "shallow". I know the word. I know it means not deep. I had no idea what it meant here, and no dictionary was going to tell me, because it is not really an English problem — it is a programming idea wearing an English word.

That is the useful distinction I found today. Some of what confuses me is vocabulary, and some of it is concepts hiding inside familiar vocabulary. When I mix them up I waste time in the wrong place, looking up words I already understand.

So I have started keeping two lists instead of one. Words I genuinely do not know go in the first. Words I know that are doing an unfamiliar job go in the second. The second list is much shorter and much more valuable.

Small side effect I did not expect: reading docs is making my written English more precise, because that is what the register demands.
