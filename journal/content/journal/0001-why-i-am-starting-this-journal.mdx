I am 34. I have never written a line of code that did anything useful. Today I am starting, and I am doing it where people can see.

The quiet version of this plan failed twice already. Both times I bought a course, watched four hours of video, felt clever, and stopped within a month. Nothing existed afterwards to prove I had ever started, so quitting cost me nothing. That is the part I want to change.

So here is the rule I am giving myself. One entry every day. Between 150 and 300 words, in English, about what I actually did and what actually confused me. Not a tutorial, not advice — a record. If the day went badly, the entry says the day went badly.

Three things happen at once this way. I learn to program. I practise English, which I need because I am aiming at remote work rather than the local market. And I build something that exists whether or not I feel motivated on a given Tuesday.

I have done a long project before. It took two years and it was about my own body, not my head, but the shape was the same: something boring repeated for far longer than felt reasonable. I know what the middle of that feels like. I am counting on that memory more than on enthusiasm.

Tomorrow I install Python. That is the whole plan for tomorrow.
