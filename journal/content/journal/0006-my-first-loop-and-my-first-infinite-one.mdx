I wrote my first `while` loop today and immediately wrote my first infinite one.

```python
count = 0
while count < 5:
    print("still going")
```

It printed forever. Thousands of lines, faster than I could read them, and my first genuine moment of panic at a computer in years — because I did not know how to make it stop. I closed the whole terminal window like someone unplugging a machine they are afraid of.

The missing line was obvious once I saw it: nothing ever changed `count`, so the condition stayed true forever. What was less obvious, and more useful, is the general shape of the mistake. A `while` loop has three parts that must agree — something that starts, something that gets checked, something that changes. I had written two of them and assumed the third.

Two things I now know that I did not this morning:

— `Ctrl-C` stops a running program. Closing the window is not required, and I am slightly embarrassed.
— A `for` loop over a range handles the changing part for you, which is presumably why everyone reaches for it first.

The panic is the part worth recording. Nothing was broken, nothing was lost, and I still reacted as if I had damaged something. I suspect a lot of early learning is just lowering the cost of being wrong until experiments feel cheap.
